\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am pleased to submit our manuscript titled \textbf{``LigPlot3D\,: An Interactive Web-Based Framework for Geometric Analysis of Protein--Ligand Interactions''} for consideration for publication as an \textbf{Application Note} in \textit{Molecular Informatics}.

Characterizing non-covalent interactions at protein--ligand interfaces is foundational to computational chemistry, molecular informatics, and structure-based drug design. With recent advances in generative chemistry and high-throughput molecular docking, candidate poses can now be generated at unprecedented scale. However, systematically dissecting and validating 3D contact geometries across candidate poses remains a significant bottleneck. Existing profilers are predominantly batch-oriented command-line utilities, 2D schematics that obscure spatial relationships, or desktop tools requiring complex local software stacks. Furthermore, server-dependent web platforms introduce latency and raise critical data-privacy concerns for proprietary chemical compounds.

LigPlot3D addresses these challenges as an open-source, client-side web framework that performs molecular parsing, geometric interaction profiling, and interactive 3D rendering entirely within the user's browser. Built on modern web standards (HTML5, JavaScript, and Three.js/WebGL), LigPlot3D operates with zero installation and ensures that structural coordinates never leave the client machine, eliminating server overhead and safeguarding confidential data. The framework deterministically detects the full spectrum of non-covalent interactions, including hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, hydrophobic contacts, $\pi$-stacking interactions, and halogen bonds, while synchronizing rich 3D spatial scenes with exportable, structured interaction tables readily usable for downstream computational analysis.

Several aspects of this work align directly with the aims and scope of \textit{Molecular Informatics}. First, rather than relying on opaque heuristics, each interaction rule is derived from established geometric standards with explicit distance and angle thresholds, providing transparent and reproducible contact assignments vital for post-docking analysis and computer-assisted molecular design. Second, we present a quantitative benchmark against an established interaction profiler (PLIP), systematically evaluating how geometric boundary formulations influence contact identification in macromolecular interfaces, alongside validation on an experimentally characterized protein--ligand complex. Finally, by eliminating local setup barriers and server dependencies, LigPlot3D provides an accessible web informatics platform that streamlines visual inspection and feature extraction for molecular docking pipelines and structure--activity relationship studies.

The tool is publicly accessible at \url{https://singhnitink.github.io/LigPlot3D/}, with all source code freely available at \url{https://github.com/singhnitink/LigPlot3D} under the MIT license. This manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not currently under consideration by any other journal. I have no conflicts of interest to declare. Thank you very much for your time and consideration of our submission.
